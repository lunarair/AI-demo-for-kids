🧠 Stop 1: The Word Brain

Your brain has about 86 billion neurons (tiny thinking cells) that are linked together.

An AI has a neural network (a pretend brain made of numbers). It learns by reading. When two words show up together a lot, the link between them gets stronger 💪, like two friends who play together every day.

🧩 Stop 2: Tokens and the Transformer

🔪 Part A: What is a token?

AI does not read letter by letter. It chops text into little pieces called tokens (the LEGO bricks of text 🧱). A token can be a whole word, part of a word, or a symbol.

Then each token gets a number. The AI only ever sees the numbers!

🍓 Why this matters: Some AIs have had trouble counting the letter "r" in "strawberry". Why? They see chunks like straw + berry, not single letters!
📏 Rule of thumb: 100 tokens is about 75 English words.

🌡️ Stop 5: What is temperature?

Temperature is a dial that controls how brave Chatty is when picking the next word.

🧊 Low temperature: always picks the most likely word. Safe, but a bit boring.
🔥 High temperature: often picks surprising words. Creative… or totally bonkers!

Temperature does not change what Chatty knows. It only changes how it picks.

✍️ Temperature on a WHOLE sentence

Chatty picks a new word again and again. At a high temperature, the silly picks add up, so a whole sentence can go totally bonkers! 🤪 Silly words are shown like this.

👩‍🏫 For grown-ups: the real maths used here 🧮
  • Each word has a score (a "logit"). The chance of each word is e^(score ÷ T) ÷ sum of e^(every score ÷ T). This is the softmax function with temperature T.
  • The bars above are computed live with this formula. At T = 0 the model simply takes the highest score (called greedy decoding).
  • Many chatbot services let developers set temperature between 0 and 1 or 0 and 2.
  • In each list, the 3 sensible words have scores 4.0, 3.4 and 2.8. The 4 silly words have scores 1.4, 1.1, 0.8 and 0.5. So the chance of a silly word in one pick is about 0% at T = 0.2, about 10% at T = 1.0 and about 28% at T = 2.0. A sentence makes 4 picks, so at T = 2.0 the chance of at least one silly word is 1 − (1 − 0.28)⁴ ≈ 73%.

👩‍🏫 For grown-ups: what is simplified here?
  • Real models do not have labelled nodes. But the idea is real: similar things ("Mars" and "Moon", "wild mushroom" and "shop mushroom", "sea water" and "bottled water") have similar internal number patterns. A very strong, common pattern can win over a weak but correct one.

😵 Oops meter (how wrong Chatty is; lower is better):

👩‍🏫 For grown-ups: the real maths used here 🧮
  • The "Oops meter" is the average cross-entropy loss: the average of −ln(chance given to the right answer). Guessing evenly across 4 answers gives −ln(0.25) ≈ 1.39.
  • Each step is one step of gradient descent: every score changes by −0.9 × (chance − correct), where correct is 1 for the right answer and 0 otherwise.
  • Real pre-training works the same way, but on trillions of tokens. For example, Meta's Llama 2 (2023) was trained on 2 trillion tokens. At about 0.75 words per token, that is about 1.5 trillion words. Reading 250 words a minute without stopping: 1.5 trillion ÷ 250 = 6 billion minutes ≈ 11,400 years.
